Four Tells Readers Clock in Four Seconds

Nobody runs your paragraph through a detector. They feel it, decide, and leave. These are the four signals doing the deciding.

Words by The Editors Spring 2025 Artificial & Human Intelligence
Field Guide

FOUR
TELLS

Method

Nobody runs your paragraph through a detector. They read a line and a half, form an opinion, and either keep going or leave. Our own reader testing put the decision at around four seconds, which is not enough time to analyse anything. It is enough time to recognise a pattern.

Four patterns do most of the damage. None of them is about vocabulary, which is where people usually start looking.

Tell 01 — The tricolon habit

Three items. Always three. Models reach for the rule of three so reliably that a page carrying four separate lists of three reads as generated before anybody finishes it.

The rule of three is genuinely good rhetoric, which is why it got learned so thoroughly and why it is now a liability. The fix is not to avoid it. The fix is to make it earn its place once per piece, and to break every other list to two items or five. Two feels decisive. Five feels observed. Three, over and over, feels like a template.

Tell 02 — The throat-clearing open

"In today's rapidly evolving landscape." "As technology continues to advance." Nobody has ever said either of these things out loud to another person.

These openings exist because the writing has to start somewhere and the real claim has not arrived yet. Cut every sentence before the first actual claim, then read what is left. In our own drafts this removes an average of two sentences from the top of every piece, and the piece is better every time, without exception, which is rare enough in editing to be worth stating flatly.

Tell 03 — The untouched template

Default fonts, default spacing, default stock photography, default corner radius. A layout nobody adjusted announces low effort before a single word has been processed, and it happens in Canva more than anywhere else because Canva is where the good templates are.

The template is not the problem. The template plus zero decisions is the problem. Change the type pairing, break one image out of its frame, and set your own margins. That is under five minutes, and it moves a design from anonymous to yours. We took this apart in full in The Untouched Template Is the Tell.

Tell 04 — Abstraction with no object

"Meaningful engagement." "Significant implications." "Robust framework." Every one of these is a noun standing where an example should be.

Swap any abstract noun for one concrete detail only you would know and the sentence becomes unfakeable. Not more impressive. Unfakeable, which is better, because it is the one quality a model cannot produce on your behalf. It does not know that your first client paid in three instalments and the third one was late.

A detail nobody else has is worth more than a sentence everybody could have written.Braincopia editorial note

Using the list

The four-second pass

Open something you published in the last month. Look only for these four, in this order, and change nothing else. Ten minutes. Then read the piece aloud, which is the only test that has ever reliably caught what the eye skips.

One warning. Hunting tells makes people timid, and timid prose fails differently but just as badly. The goal is not writing that passes an inspection. It is writing that could only have come from you, and the four tells are just the places your work most often stops being yours.
